The 1th Counseling
Client Profile:
Static Traits: ## User Background
[Personal Profile]  
Ruth is a recent college graduate now adjusting to her new role as a full-time homemaker, which feels daunting as she contemplates her evolving identity. She is very conscious of her age and worried about external perceptions.

[Personal Characteristics]  
Ruth enjoys gardening, a hobby she previously engaged in before her schedule became hectic, and is considering returning to it as a means of relaxation.

[Assessment and Diagnosis]  
Ruth experiences overwhelming anxiety related to her transition and critiques her adequacy in her new role, although there is no indication of a formal mental health diagnosis.
Counseling Memory:
[Emotion and Cognitive State:] Ruth feels overwhelmed and anxious due to her transition from student to homemaker and struggles with a lost sense of identity.
[Counselor Observations:] The therapist notes Ruth's intense pressure from trying to redefine herself and her openness to exploring ways to reduce anxiety, identifying her self-awareness as an asset.
[Counseling Assignments:] Ruth should jot down anxious thoughts and emotions as they arise to review and explore these in subsequent sessions.
[Session Goal:] Utilize CBT strategies to help Ruth manage anxiety by identifying irrational thoughts and exploring realistic self-perceptions through her daily experiences.
Counseling Summary:
Ruth explored the challenges of transitioning from being a college student to a full-time homemaker, particularly regarding feelings of inadequacy and anxiety about perceived judgment. Through the counseling sessions, she learned to recognize and adjust negative thought patterns, practice self-kindness, and engage in supportive conversations with loved ones, while also rediscovering joy through hobbies like gardening. These strategies have empowered Ruth to maintain a balanced perspective and seek support, enhancing her overall well-being and self-confidence.
