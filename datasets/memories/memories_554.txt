The 1th Counseling
Client Profile:
Static Traits: ## User Background
[Personal Profile]  
C is a university student who is currently experiencing a lot of pressure trying to adapt to university life, particularly due to the overwhelming workload, which has exacerbated obsessive thoughts and anxiety.

[Personal Characteristics]
C enjoys reading as a way to unwind, although they have found it difficult to make time for it lately.

[Assessment and Diagnosis]  
C experiences anxiety, particularly when confronted with academic pressures and obsessive thoughts; there is no formal diagnosis of a mental health disorder.
Counseling Memory:
[Emotion and Cognitive State:] C feels overwhelmed and anxious about the university workload and a perceived lack of control over their situation.
[Counselor Observations:] The consultant notices C's struggle with maintaining control and the tendency to panic under pressure, alongside a willingness to explore new coping mechanisms.
[Counseling Assignments:] C should keep a journal of daily thoughts to identify recurring patterns and integrate reading as a short, recreational break to mitigate stress.
[Session Goal:] To help C recognize and question obsessive thoughts using CBT strategies, aiming to enhance their control over stressful academic demands.
Counseling Summary:
The session explored C's anxiety stemming from university pressures and obsessive thoughts, focusing on the feeling of losing control. The consultant recommended keeping a journal to identify thought patterns, using reminders to take breaks, and incorporating mindfulness and reading into daily routines to manage stress. C agreed to try these strategies and plans to discuss bothersome thought patterns in future sessions.
