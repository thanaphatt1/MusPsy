The 1th Counseling
Client Profile:
Static Traits: ## User Background
[Personal Profile]  
Zhang is a student facing a significant decision between pursuing postgraduate studies in psychology or philosophy. The choice is causing considerable stress and anxiety, particularly given the influence of parents who favor philosophy for its perceived better job prospects. Zhang has also expressed a longstanding interest in psychology due to a curiosity about human behavior.

[Personal Characteristics]  
Zhang enjoys playing the piano, an activity that serves as a personal outlet for relaxation and stress relief. 

[Assessment and Diagnosis]  
Zhang experiences anxiety related to decision-making and parental expectations, but there is no formal diagnosis of a mental health disorder.
Counseling Memory:
[Emotion and Cognitive State:] Zhang feels trapped in a cycle of indecision and anxiety, significantly impacted by the pressure to meet parental expectations and fears about the future.
[Counselor Observations:] The therapist observes Zhang's engagement in deeply exploring their interests and motivations, alongside a readiness to address anxiety triggers through reflection.
[Counseling Assignments:] Zhang should reflect on personal motivations for both prospective fields of study and jot down recurring anxious thoughts to see patterns and impact.
[Session Goal:] Assist Zhang in identifying true interests and motivations, guiding them to consider personal excitement over external expectations using CBT strategies.
Counseling Summary:
The dialogue explored Zhang's anxiety over choosing between psychology and philosophy for postgraduate study, influenced by both personal interests and parental expectations. The consultant encouraged Zhang to identify and challenge recurrent anxious thoughts, relate decision-making to his passion for the piano, and visualize supportive scenarios with his parents. The process of reflecting on intrinsic motivations and externalizing thoughts aimed to provide Zhang clarity and confidence in decision-making.
