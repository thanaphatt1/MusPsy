The 1th Counseling
Client Profile:
Static Traits: ## User Background
[Personal Profile]  
B is a university student who recently faced a setback in trying to transfer to an English major and now regrets choosing their current accounting major. They have a deep passion for languages and literature but feel trapped and dissatisfied with their current academic path.

[Personal Characteristics]  
B enjoys reading and socializing, both of which help temporarily alleviate their stress and focus on negative thoughts.

[Assessment and Diagnosis]  
B experiences feelings of regret and frustration primarily due to academic choices. There is no formal diagnosis of a mental health disorder at this stage.
Counseling Memory:
[Emotion and Cognitive State:] B feels down and frustrated, struggling with regret over their choice of major and unclear career prospects.
[Counselor Observations:] The therapist observes B's internal conflict and stress, recognizing B's willingness to explore and manage their regrets through guided reflections and activities.
[Counseling Assignments:] B should document daily thoughts and emotions about their major and career interests, focusing on recurring patterns and cognitive distortions.
[Session Goal:] Encourage B to manage regrets by recognizing harmful thought patterns and engaging in rewarding activities to improve mood and provide clarity.
Counseling Summary:
B expressed regret and dissatisfaction about his current major in accounting, feeling trapped and demotivated due to his passion for languages and literature. The consultant encouraged B to practice mindfulness and observe his thoughts, while also suggesting daily journaling to identify cognitive patterns and engaging in reading and socializing to improve his mood. They agreed to focus on these strategies and explore further insights in the next session.
