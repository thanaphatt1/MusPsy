The 1th Counseling
Client Profile:
Static Traits: ## User Background
[Personal Profile]  
Chen is a first-year university student who hoped for a fresh start but finds themselves feeling more anxious in the new environment. They struggle with past experiences that impact their confidence, particularly with approaching professors, stemming from a troublesome incident involving a teacher and gaming habits in high school.

[Personal Characteristics]  
Chen enjoys photography, which serves as both a creative outlet and a method for relaxation.

[Assessment and Diagnosis]  
Chen experiences anxiety primarily related to academic interactions and social settings. There is no formal diagnosis of a mental health disorder mentioned.
Counseling Memory:
[Emotion and Cognitive State:] Chen feels anxious and out of place due to past negative experiences influencing current academic and social interactions.
[Counselor Observations:] The therapist notes that Chen’s anxiety is linked to an apprehension of judgment and rejection, especially from authority figures, yet sees a willingness in Chen to explore and manage these fears.
[Counseling Assignments:] Chen is encouraged to record thoughts and emotions when they occur to identify anxiety patterns and triggers.
[Session Goal:] Utilize CBT strategies to help Chen identify and challenge anxiety-driven thoughts and perceptions about academic interactions.
Counseling Summary:
In the counseling dialogue, Chen explores how past experiences have heightened his anxiety about interacting with university professors. By identifying thoughts and emotions tied to these interactions, Chen is encouraged to challenge his perceptions with evidence and shift his mindset to see these encounters as opportunities for learning rather than judgment. The dialogue concludes with setting manageable goals such as initiating small interactions with professors, leveraging social support from friends, and tracking positive experiences to build confidence and alleviate anxiety.
