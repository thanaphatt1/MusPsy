The 1th Counseling
Client Profile:
Static Traits: ## User Background
[Personal Profile]  
The user is a graduate student who recently transitioned to a new academic environment and is experiencing psychological distress from past interactions with classmates. The user is striving to adjust while dealing with anxiety and a lack of confidence in social scenarios.

[Personal Characteristics]
The user enjoys playing badminton as a leisure activity and finds their brother to be a significant source of emotional support.

[Assessment and Diagnosis]  
The user shows signs of social anxiety, characterized by fear and avoidance behaviors in social situations, influenced by past experiences. There is no formal diagnosis of a mental health disorder mentioned.
Counseling Memory:
[Emotion and Cognitive State:] The user feels overwhelmed and anxious, especially when interacting with classmates, due to past misunderstandings that have affected their confidence.
[Counselor Observations:] The therapist notices the user's avoidance in social settings and recognizes the impact of previous experiences on their current state of mind, seeing openness to exploring coping strategies.
[Counseling Assignments:] The user is tasked with observing and noting their feelings during social interactions and sharing these reflections with their brother for additional perspective.
[Session Goal:] Foster the user's awareness of thoughts arising in social settings to manage anxiety more effectively, encouraging the use of supportive dialogues and gradual social engagement.
Counseling Summary:
Explored the user's social anxiety stemming from past misunderstandings with peers, leading to avoidance behavior in current social situations at graduate school. Recommended observing and noting down feelings during social interactions to identify triggers, and encouraged experimenting with small social tasks to build confidence. Suggested leveraging support from the user's brother for encouragement and perspective, with a plan to discuss findings and reflections in the next session.
